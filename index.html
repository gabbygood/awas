Automatic Water Agri System

In the world of agriculture, the efficient use of water resources has always been a priority. With climate change and rising water scarcity, farmers are increasingly adopting automatic water irrigation systems to optimize water usage, boost crop yield, and reduce labor. These systems, equipped with sensors and controllers, enable the precise distribution of water according to plant needs, ensuring that no water is wasted. By automating irrigation, farmers can focus on other critical tasks, monitor water usage remotely, and ensure their crops get just the right amount of moisture. This modern approach is transforming agricultural practices, making them more sustainable and productive.

Arduino Board

Arduino UNO or any compatible model. This acts as the central controller for the system.

Soil Moisture Sensors

These sensors measure the moisture content of the soil and send readings to the Arduino.

Water Pump

A 5V or 12V submersible water pump is often used to draw water and distribute it to the plants.

REVENUE MODEL FOR SOCIAL & NON -SOCIAL ENTERPRISES

By Mr. Karl Dominic Fajardo

What is a Social Enterprise? A social enterprise focuses on achieving social or environmental goals while simultaneously generating revenue. These organizations aim to make a positive impact on society or the environment.

What is a Non-Social Enterprise? In contrast, non-social enterprises prioritize profit generation without an inherent focus on social missions. Their primary objective is financial gain.

Why Revenue Models Matter Revenue models shape the sustainability and growth potential of any enterprise. They: Guide financial planning. Influence resource allocation. Drive strategic decision-making. Without a strong revenue model: Businesses risk failure. For example, tech startups relying solely on one revenue source often collapse during market shifts. Key Insight: “Diverse and strategic revenue streams mitigate risks and sustain operations during market changes.”

Revenue Models in Non-Social Enterprises

Traditional Models: Direct Sales: Selling products/services directly to customers. Subscription Services: Generating recurring income through memberships. Pros and Cons: Direct sales offer immediate cash flow but can be inconsistent. Subscriptions provide stability but require high customer retention. Innovative Models: Freemium: Offering basic services for free with paid premium upgrades. Pay-as-you-go: Pricing based on usage, common in SaaS (Software as a Service).

Reflection: Revenue models are crucial for any enterprise, including non-social ones like SYNCHRO's AWAS, which focuses on delivering innovative solutions for automated water management. As a non-social enterprise, AWAS prioritizes profitability while addressing customer needs with a cutting-edge product. Insights from traditional and innovative revenue models highlight potential paths for AWAS to generate income effectively. For instance, adopting a direct sales model can provide immediate cash flow, while incorporating subscription-based features, such as advanced analytics or remote control functionalities, could create recurring revenue streams.

          Total Addressable Market (TAM)

This represents the global demand for AWAS if everyone who needs it uses it.

Who’s included?
Households, agricultural businesses, and plant enthusiasts worldwide.
Key facts:
The global market for smart irrigation systems was valued at $2.5 billion in 2023 and is growing by 15% per year.
There are 2.5 billion people in cities worldwide who could potentially grow plants or gardens.
Globally, there are 570 million farms that may benefit from automation like AWAS.
Estimate:
About 2 billion plant growers (households + businesses) globally.
Each would spend about $100 per year on an automated watering system.
TAM = $200 billion/year.
